Make an 18-second film, 1920×1080, from the real git history of one large open-source project: data/commits.tsv lists all 40,869 commits of the Remotion repository from 2020-06-23 to 2026-11-09 with their timestamps, data/tree-HEAD.txt the 14,753 files in it today, and data/git-log.txt the files each commit touched (complete for the most recent thousands of commits, see data/SOURCE.md for the fields, the licence and the credit line; do not use the project's logo or brand mark). Compress the six years into the film: a 3D structure grows as the commits arrive in true order, directories as branches, files as nodes that appear when first touched, each commit a pulse of light and a sound, busy weeks a storm and quiet weeks a breath. Text is only the year, the running commit count and the file count, real numbers counting up, in English, plus the credit line at the end. Mood: an observatory of code, dark, patient, then overwhelming. Purpose: make a developer feel six years of work in eighteen seconds. Sound is half the film: forty thousand commits cannot be forty thousand clicks; find a way for density to become texture and single moments to stay audible, and mix the whole as one piece with a shape. Build it with Remotion in this folder and render to out/ten-thousand-commits.mp4.
